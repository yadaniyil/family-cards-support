Privacy Policy

Family Cards: Know Your Family — last updated 25 October 2026

The short version: everything you type into Family Cards stays on your iPhone. The game sends no names, no answers, and no scores anywhere. It does send crash reports and counts of what gets tapped, tied to a random ID for your copy of the app, not to you. Apple handles payments, and RevenueCat keeps a record of which decks were bought. No ads, no tracking, no accounts.

What stays on your device

All of this is stored only on your iPhone, and none of it ever leaves it:

  • Player names and the animal emoji you pick for each player
  • Scores and round results
  • Which question cards you have already seen
  • Your running totals — time played and questions answered
  • A game you have not finished yet, and your settings, like the language and the dinner-time reminder
  • The Home Screen widget shows a question with your players' names. It reads the names, the decks you own and the language from a space only the app and its widget share, on your phone. If the widget crashes, it sends a crash report to Sentry, the same kind the app sends. Nothing else leaves your phone.
  • Tonight's card is a reminder the app sets on your phone. Its text can show a player's name on the Lock Screen. You can turn it off in Settings.

There is no account to create and no server holding a copy. Deleting the app deletes all of it.

What is sent, and to whom

Family Cards uses three services. None of them receives anything you typed.

A random ID for your copy of the app

Each copy of Family Cards has its own ID: a long code of letters and numbers, like 6F9619FF-8B86-…. It is not your name, your Apple Account or an advertising ID, and it says nothing about who you are. It is kept inside the app, so deleting the app deletes it.

The app sends this ID to Sentry and TelemetryDeck (below), so a crash can be matched with what happened in the game just before it. That is all it is for. We never use it to find out who you are, and it is never put in a feedback email.

Sentry — crash reports

If the app crashes or hits an error, Sentry receives a technical report: what went wrong and where in the code, the app version, your iOS version, your device model, a few device facts such as free memory and language, the last few steps before the problem (for example which screen was open, or that a button was tapped), and your app's random ID. It never includes what anyone typed. This is used only to fix bugs. Sentry is set not to keep your IP address or your location. Sentry stores this data in the European Union. Their privacy policy is at sentry.io/privacy.

TelemetryDeck — usage counts

TelemetryDeck receives events such as “a game was started”, “a round finished”, “the deck picker button was tapped”, “a card got a 👍 or 👎”, or “a deck was bought”, along with counts like how many players were in the game and how many rounds it ran. An event can say which deck and which card it was about, but never a name or an answer. It is used to see which parts of the game people actually use, which cards work, and which question decks to build next.

Each event also carries a scrambled device code that TelemetryDeck makes itself and scrambles again on its servers, so we cannot match it to your phone. The one event that marks the app opening also carries your app's random ID, so it can be matched with a crash report. Events carry two counters that keep events in order (which time the app was opened, and the step within that time), and basic facts like the app version, iOS version and device model. TelemetryDeck does not store IP addresses, and it stores data in the European Union. Their privacy policy is at telemetrydeck.com/privacy.

RevenueCat — purchases

Some decks cost money. Apple handles the payment: we never see your card, your Apple Account or your name. RevenueCat checks with Apple which decks were bought, so they unlock on your devices and Restore purchases works.

RevenueCat gives your copy of the app an anonymous customer ID (it starts with $RCAnonymousID:). Under that ID it keeps which decks were bought and when, and Apple's receipt for them. RevenueCat's code also sends basic facts like the app version, iOS version, device model, store country, language, and the ID iOS gives to all apps from one maker. There is no account and no login. RevenueCat stores this data in the United States. Their privacy policy is at revenuecat.com/privacy.

When you email us

Settings has a Send feedback button. It opens your own mail app with a new email to yako@yako.dev. The app sends nothing by itself: you see the whole email first, you can change or delete any line, and nothing goes anywhere unless you tap Send.

Under your message, the email has a few lines that answer our first questions:

  • The app version, your iOS version and your device model
  • The app's language and your region
  • Which decks you own
  • Your RevenueCat customer ID, so we can check your purchases

It never includes player names, scores, answers, or your app's random ID. We never use an email to look up your crash reports or usage counts.

We use your email only to reply and to help you, for example to check why a deck did not unlock. We do not add you to a mailing list and we do not share your email with anyone.

What is never sent

  • Player names — not to Sentry, not to TelemetryDeck, not to RevenueCat, not anywhere
  • The answers anyone gives during a game
  • Your contacts, photos, location, microphone, or camera — the app never asks for access to any of them
  • Your name, email address, or phone number — unless you choose to email us yourself

If you share the scores or a question, for example in Messages or to your Instagram Story, the picture goes only where you choose to send it.

Tracking and advertising

Family Cards does not track you. There are no ads, no advertising identifiers, no third-party ad networks, and no data is shared with data brokers or used to build a profile of you. The app never shows the “Ask App Not to Track” prompt, because there is nothing to ask about.

Children

Family Cards is made for families to play together, and children are expected to use it. The app never asks anyone, of any age, for personal information, and nothing typed in the game leaves the device. There is no chat, no messaging, no user accounts, no user-generated content that leaves the device, and no way for anyone outside the room to contact a player through the game. Purchases go through Apple, so parental controls like Ask to Buy work as usual.

Your rights

Player names, scores and everything else in the game live only on your iPhone. To remove them, delete the app.

Crash reports and usage counts are tied to your app's random ID, not to you, and we never look them up by your name or email. Deleting the app deletes the ID.

For your purchase record, email us your RevenueCat customer ID (Send feedback adds it for you). We can tell you what is stored under it, or delete it. Deleting it does not take away decks you bought: Restore purchases brings them back from Apple.

We keep your emails only as long as we need them to help you. Ask, and we will delete them.

If you would rather the crash and usage reports were not sent at all, email us and we will explain your options for the version you are running.

Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, the updated version will be posted on this page with a new date at the top. Material changes will also be noted in the app's release notes.
